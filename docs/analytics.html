At a glance

How we count. Every figure counts judgments, not applications. A judgment that decides several Articles counts under each, so the Article bars do not add up to the total, and a judgment against several States counts once for each State. Committee judgments are included; admissibility decisions are not, nor are the 31 Grand Chamber decisions that Search holds. Methodology

Outcomes by respondent State

Every respondent State, in the columns of the Court's own table Violations by Article and by State as far as the HUDOC metadata allow. Click a column heading to sort.

Violation rate = judgments with at least one violation ÷ all judgments against the State. Other = judgments with no violation or no-violation tag that neither strike out nor record a friendly settlement: mostly just-satisfaction judgments, and some whose HUDOC record lacks the outcome tag. States with fewer than 20 judgments are greyed: their rates rest on too few cases.

Outcomes by Convention Article

Judgments with a violation or no-violation tag for each Article (subparagraphs count under their Article). Bar length is the number of such judgments (n), colours split it by outcome, and the label gives n · share with at least one violation.

Judgments delivered each year by the Grand Chamber, Chambers and three-judge Committees; the bar height is the year's total.

Dashed lines: Protocol No. 11 in force, 1 November 1998 (the single full-time Court); Protocol No. 14 in force, 1 June 2010 (Committees begin to give judgments on the merits); Russia ceases to be a party to the Convention, 16 September 2022. Coverage, unresolved matches and methodology

This is a metadata-based ranking, not our own full-text citation count (the extracted application numbers are HUDOC's, without citation context). Counts are lower bounds from resolved references; missing or ambiguous references can affect ordering. Application number alone is insufficient when several judgments are possible. Unresolved references are not guessed. Citing and cited documents must both be in the judgment catalog; cited decisions are excluded.

Inadmissibility and strike-outs in judgments

Judgments whose conclusion declares part of the application inadmissible or strikes it out of the list, and the inadmissibility grounds the conclusion names.

Admissibility decisions are outside this corpus, so this is not the Court's filtering rate: most applications are declared inadmissible or struck out by a decision and never reach a judgment. How to read this

Paragraph distribution

Non-empty text rows by section of the judgment, from the production texts and the local English and French source bundles. Header and applicant-table rows are included, so this is not a count of numbered paragraphs. Methodology

French texts are included; section labels in the newly parsed bundles have not yet had source-exact checks, and applicant tables can dominate the Appendix count.

Coverage and languages

How many judgments carry each metadata field, and the language of the texts. Availability, not an accuracy score: a blank field does not mean the judgment does not discuss the subject. Methodology

Language of inventoried judgment texts

An English metadata flag does not prove that an English text exists. Older production texts have no recorded source language and stay unknown rather than guessed. French bundles are original texts, not translations made by this tool.
